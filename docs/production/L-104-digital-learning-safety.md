# L-104 — Digital Learning and Internet Safety

**Status: production draft only.** This document is an original lesson script and recording plan. It is not a transcript of recorded media, not a timed caption file, and not available from the API. The planned cut is **2 minutes 30 seconds (150 seconds)**; the real runtime must be measured after recording and editing. Do not publish a lesson manifest or offline pack until the final media and synchronized captions exist.

## Learning goals

By the end, a learner should be able to check a learning-site address, protect sign-in information, make safer choices about downloads and sharing, and verify an online claim.

## English narration master script

### Scene 1 — Study in different places

Imagine two learners preparing for class. One studies at home on a phone; another uses a laptop in a campus library. Online lessons can share notes, recorded explanations, and practice. In Addis Ababa, Hawassa, Mekelle, or a rural study group, the same checks can help keep learning safer.

### Scene 2 — Check the learning site

Before signing in, check the full website address. A familiar logo alone does not prove the page belongs to your school. Open the course from a trusted school link or ask your teacher. Stop if the address is misspelled, the browser shows a warning, or the page asks for information unrelated to the lesson.

### Scene 3 — Protect your account

Keep your password and one-time sign-in codes private, even from someone who contacts you unexpectedly. Use a different password for your learning account. Lock a shared phone or computer when you step away. Turn on a second sign-in check if your school offers one, and never save your password on a public computer.

### Scene 4 — Use data and downloads carefully

If mobile data is limited, save it by downloading the lesson when a trusted Wi-Fi connection is available. Before opening a file, check its name and confirm your teacher shared it. Captions and transcripts can help when sound is low or you need to review a term.

### Scene 5 — Pause before sharing

A photo, full name, class link, or mark can reveal more than you intend. Choose who can see a post. Ask permission before sharing another learner’s work or image, and keep private class details out of public groups. If a message urgently asks for money, a code, or a password reset, do not reply through it. Contact your teacher using a trusted channel.

### Scene 6 — Check what you learn

Look for the author and date. Compare important claims with your course materials and another reliable source. Ask when something is unclear. Digital tools support learning, but they do not replace careful thinking. Check the address, protect your account, review downloads, and pause before sharing. These small steps help make learning safer for everyone.

**Recording note:** The English narration is 319 whitespace-delimited words, or about 128 words per minute at a 150-second target before allowing for pauses. It is suitable as a 2–3 minute narration draft, not a duration claim. Time a natural read with the planned visuals and pauses. If the edit is short, add useful instruction rather than stretching words or assigning timestamps before recording. Create the final English transcript from the locked recording.
